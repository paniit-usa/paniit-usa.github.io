Show & Tell Guidelines

Suggestions for preparing a short talk and demo for a PANIIT Tech Show & Tell panel session.

Preparing your talk

These suggestions come from what has worked well in past sessions. They help every speaker get a fair share of time and keep the session lively.

  • Please keep your talk to 15 minutes at most. This is a firm limit: it is the only way we can fit three talks into one hour. The timekeeper will give you a signal when 5 minutes remain and again when 1 minute remains.
  • Keep it to around 5 slides, with a large font (24 pt or larger) so they are easy to read on screen.
  • Skip the "about me" slide. The moderator will introduce each speaker, so you can dive straight into your topic.
  • Introduce the topic in simple terms within the first couple of minutes, so everyone in the audience can follow along.
  • Start with an overview, then show the demo. A little context makes the demo much easier to appreciate.
  • Aim to keep the demo to 6 minutes or less. If you are using Colab, it helps to run the cells before the session starts.
  • Have a recorded backup of your demo, just in case something goes wrong with the live version.
  • Keep the focus educational rather than promoting a product or company.
  • Close with one key message you would like the audience to remember.
  • Share your code from your own GitHub repository if you like; we are happy to add a link to it.
  • Keep API keys and private data off the screen. All sessions are recorded for our YouTube channel.
  • Send us your slides (as a PDF) about 2 days before the session, so we can have everything ready.
One way to structure 15 minutes
Time (min)SectionWhat to cover
0–1OpeningYour topic, and the one idea you want people to leave with
1–3Why it mattersThe problem or question, and who it affects
3–7Your approachWhat you built, tried, or learned: the key idea, not every detail
7–12Make it concreteA demo, results, a before-and-after, a case study, or a worked example
12–14TakeawayYour key message, lessons learned, and where to find more
14–15BufferBreathing room for transitions or anything that runs long

Treat this as a starting point and adjust the timings to fit your talk.

Q&A after each talk

Each talk has its own short Q&A, so questions stay specific and the talk is still fresh in everyone's mind.

  • Each Q&A runs for about 3 minutes. At the 3-minute mark the moderator wraps up, so the next talk can start on time.
  • Attendees post their questions in the chat during the talk.
  • The moderator picks 2–3 questions from the chat for each Q&A.
  • The moderator also prepares one question for each speaker, in case the chat is quiet.
  • The moderator introduces the next speaker during the last 30 seconds of each Q&A.
  • Any unanswered questions are sent to the speakers after the session, so they can follow up if they wish.
